Heavy Rain Disrupts Mumbai; Orange Alert Issued

Heavy rainfall lashed Mumbai on Tuesday morning, bringing large parts of the city to a standstill.
Local train services on the Central line were suspended for nearly three hours after tracks between Kurla and Sion were flooded.
The India Meteorological Department (IMD) has issued an orange alert for Mumbai and Thane for the next two days, warning of very heavy rainfall at isolated places.
The Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) announced that all schools and colleges in the city would remain closed on Wednesday as a precaution.
According to the IMD, the Santacruz observatory recorded 145 mm of rain in the 12 hours ending at 8.30 a.m.
Several low-lying areas, including Andheri subway and Hindmata, reported severe waterlogging, forcing traffic police to divert vehicles.
"We have deployed additional pumps at 30 flood-prone locations and our teams are working round the clock," a senior BMC official said.
Flights at Mumbai airport were delayed by an average of 40 minutes, though no cancellations were reported.
Officials advised citizens to stay indoors unless travel was absolutely necessary.
Weather experts said the heavy rain was caused by a low-pressure area over the Bay of Bengal combined with an active monsoon trough.
The Mumbai Police also urged people to avoid the sea front, where high tides of up to 4.5 metres are expected on Wednesday afternoon.
